## Where I Differ

### 1. Assumption of Physicality
The argument assumes that any "higher-level" host must be bound by the same physical constraints as our universe (P4). However, this assumption may not hold if:
- The host universe operates under different physical laws or
- The simulation is not running on a traditional "computer" but some other information-processing system we haven't conceived.

We currently have no evidence for or against such possibilities, but they cannot be ruled out a priori.

### 2. Fidelity vs. Experience
The argument focuses on physical fidelity. However, a simulation need not reproduce every microstate to create a subjectively real experience. As noted in the "on-demand rendering" objection, only the regions actively being observed need high fidelity. While the original argument correctly points out that maintaining consistency across all possible observation paths would still require near-physical fidelity, this creates a testable prediction: observable anomalies (e.g., "seams" or inconsistencies in spacelike-separated regions).

### 3. Information-Theoretic Interpretation of "Host"
The argument assumes a traditional, physical host. However, information theory does not strictly require a physical substrate for computation. A "host" could be:
- A purely mathematical substrate,
- A different kind of information-processing system, or
- A system operating at a qualitatively different scale (e.g., leveraging quantum or other non-classical resources).

### 4. Cosmological Assumptions
The entropy bound (P3) assumes our understanding of cosmic inflation and de Sitter horizons is complete. While this is currently our best framework, it may be revised by future discoveries in quantum gravity or cosmology.
